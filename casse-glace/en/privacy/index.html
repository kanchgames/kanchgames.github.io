Privacy policy

Casse-Glace, a game by Kanch Games

Last updated: October 27, 2026 (version 1.2.0 of the game)

In short

Casse-Glace asks for no account and we collect no personal data: your progress stays on your device, and the whole game can be played offline. It shows ads suited to children and never personalized, served by Google AdMob; to load them, Google's library sends Google some technical data (see “Advertising”). The optional “Casse-Glace Full” purchase removes the ads; it goes through Google Play (see “Purchases”).

No account, nothing collected by us

The game asks for no account, no sign-up and no email address. We collect no personal data and the game sends us nothing: it contains no analytics, audience measurement or tracking tool of ours. Only Google's advertising library sends data to Google, described under “Advertising”.

Permissions

The game asks Android for these permissions:

  • internet access (INTERNET) and the network's state (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE), only to load the ads: the game itself does not need them and plays offline;
  • two technical permissions added by Google's advertising library, for its background tasks (Android's WorkManager): keeping the processor awake during a task (WAKE_LOCK) and a foreground service (FOREGROUND_SERVICE, which the game never starts); they give access to no data;
  • vibration (VIBRATE), for small vibrations while playing (they can be turned off in the game's settings);
  • Google Play billing (com.android.vending.BILLING), which lets it offer the optional purchase through the device's Google Play app.

The game does not ask for the advertising ID (the AD_ID permission): Android does not give it to the game. Nor does it ask for the permissions of Android's “Privacy Sandbox” (interest-based ads and attribution measurement).

What is saved on your device

So that you can pick up where you left off, the game saves, only in its own storage on your device:

  • your progress: puzzles solved and their stars, daily challenge history and streaks, crystals, hints, whether the tutorial was seen;
  • your settings (sound, vibration, view, language, graphics quality, mood…);
  • the game in progress, to resume it;
  • for multiplayer, the first names you choose to type for the players (optional: otherwise “Player 1”, “Player 2”…) and the multiplayer game in progress;
  • if you made it, the “Casse-Glace Full” purchase: only the fact that you own it;
  • to limit the ads: the number of levels won since the last full-screen ad and its time, the number of videos watched that day.

This data stays on the device: we have no access to it. Android's automatic cloud backup is turned off for this game; the data only leaves the device if you copy it yourself straight to a new device (Android's device-to-device transfer).

Deleting this data

Uninstalling the game deletes all of this data; a purchase stays linked to your Google account and is restored when you reinstall the game. You can also erase your progress at any time from the game's settings (“Reset progress”); your settings and your purchase are then kept.

Children and families

Casse-Glace is for everyone, children included. It is designed in line with Google Play's policies for apps for children and families. Links that leave the game (this policy, the contact email) and the purchase are behind a parental gate: a multiplication to solve. Whatever the player's age, ads are always requested as for a child (see “Advertising”).

Advertising

The game is free and partly funded by ads, served by Google AdMob, a Google library that Google Play accepts for apps for families (its program of self-certified ads SDKs).

Where and when.

  • A banner at the bottom of the home screen, the levels' track and the settings; never during a game, never over a button.
  • Now and then, a full-screen ad between two levels, after a short break: at most one every 5 levels won, at least 3 minutes apart; never during the tutorial, the “Discovery” chapter or the daily challenge, nor during a new player's first 10 levels.
  • A video, only if the player chooses to watch it, for 3 crystals (10 videos a day at most).

Ads for children, for everyone. The game does not ask for anyone's age: every ad request is flagged to Google as directed to a child, with content rated “G” (general audiences) at most. According to Google, this treatment turns off personalized ads and remarketing, turns off requests to third-party ad vendors (measurement pixels, third-party ad servers), applies its protections for children, and the Android advertising ID is not sent (the game cannot read it anyway).

What Google's library sends to Google, according to Google (Google's disclosure for the Play Store), to show the ads, measure them and fight fraud:

  • the device's IP address, which may be used to estimate its approximate location;
  • interactions with the ads and the app (app launch, taps, videos watched);
  • diagnostic information (launch time, hangs, energy use);
  • device identifiers: Android's app set ID; the advertising ID is not sent.

This data is encrypted in transit. Google processes it under its own policies: how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services, advertising at Google and Google's privacy policy. The library also keeps what it needs on the device (cached ads, for instance). We only get overall statistics from Google (number of ads shown, revenue), never data about a player.

No consent asked for. Since no personalized ad is ever requested, the game shows no consent form; in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google then serves “limited ads” there (according to Google, without collecting, sharing or using personal data to personalize ads; the IP address is still used to deliver the ad to the device). We have left AdMob's “programmatic limited ads” turned on (its default setting): they let these limited ads, always contextual, come from Google's programmatic demand and the buyers it authorizes; for them, Google uses cookies and local storage on the device for invalid traffic detection only, to defend against fraud and abuse, a use for which Google does not require consent (limited ads, according to Google).

Legal basis. These ads rest on our legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR): contextual ads, never personalized, that fund a free game, with no tracking or profiling, and that stop after the “Casse-Glace Full” purchase (only the video remains offered, if the player chooses to watch it).

Your right to object. You can object to this processing at any time, in two ways. The one-time “Casse-Glace Full” purchase removes the ads: no banner, no full-screen ad, and Google's library is then not even started, unless the player chooses to watch a video to earn crystals. Or play offline: without a connection, no ad loads and nothing is sent.

Purchases

The game offers a single, optional one-time purchase, “Casse-Glace Full”, which unlocks multiplayer and removes the ads. It always goes through the parental gate.

  • Payment is handled only by Google Play, in its own screens, under Google's privacy policy. We neither receive nor store any payment data. Google Play only gives us the order details used to track sales (order number, date, product, price, country).
  • Only the purchased right is kept on your device, so that multiplayer also works offline.
  • The game checks this right with Google Play, through the device's Google Play app (at start-up, when you come back to the game, when you open multiplayer): this is how a purchase is restored after reinstalling or on another device with the same Google account, and removed if it is refunded. “Restore my purchases”, in the settings, runs the same check on request.
  • No server of ours is involved: the game sends us nothing.
  • Google's billing library contains a module that would send its own diagnostics: it is turned off in the game (its events are deleted on the device, never sent).
